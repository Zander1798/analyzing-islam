Foreword
What this is

This book is a reference catalog of passages from Islam's foundational texts that present philosophical, historical, moral, or logical difficulties. Every entry cites a specific verse or hadith, explains what it says in plain language, and builds the case for why it presents a problem. Where relevant, entries also present the standard Muslim apologetic response and push back on it.

Volume I covers three sources: the Quran, Sahih al-Bukhari, and Sahih Muslim — the texts that Sunni Islam itself ranks as most authoritative. No fringe collections. No hostile translations. The case is built entirely from Islam's own mainstream tradition.

How entries are organized

The book is divided into three parts, one per source. Within each part, entries are grouped into thematic chapters — Abrogation, Contradictions, Warfare & Jihad, and so on. Each chapter collects every entry from that source that belongs to that theme. If a source has no entries in a given category, that chapter is omitted entirely.

When an entry touches more than one theme, it appears under the category that best captures its primary problem. It does not appear twice.

How to read an entry

Each entry contains four elements:

Reference The source citation — verse number, hadith number, or book and chapter.
Rating The apologetic difficulty level: Basic, Moderate, or Strong.
Passage The text quoted in full from the standard translation.
Commentary An explanation of the problem — what it says, why it matters, and where the standard apologetic falls short.

Strength ratings

Every entry is rated according to how difficult the problem is to answer from within the Islamic apologetic tradition:

Basic Apologists have a stock reply. The problem is real but the standard response is widely known and rehearsed. A reader who has followed Islamic apologetics will have heard the answer.
Moderate Answering requires conceding something — softening a claim, reinterpreting a text, or acknowledging that the tradition is not unanimous. The problem cannot be dismissed outright.
Strong The apologetic moves themselves generate new problems. Every standard response either contradicts another Islamic claim, requires abandoning the plain meaning of the text, or introduces a difficulty worse than the original.

Ratings reflect apologetic difficulty — not moral severity. A passage can be morally disturbing and still rated Basic if the apologetic reply is well-established and coherent. Conversely, an apparently minor textual quirk may rate Strong if no clean answer exists.

Sources and translations

Quranic verses are quoted from the Saheeh International English translation — the Saudi-sanctioned mainstream Sunni edition, widely used in mosques and Islamic universities across the English-speaking world. Choosing the most mainstream translation removes the easy dismissal of "hostile translation."

Hadiths are quoted from widely published English translations of the same collections that Sunni jurisprudence itself treats as authoritative: Sahih al-Bukhari and Sahih Muslim in this volume. Hadith numbers follow the standard USC-MSA / sunnah.com numbering used in English-language Islamic scholarship. Every passage can be independently verified.

A note on tone

This catalog does not argue. It presents. The entries speak through the texts themselves — the reader is left to draw their own conclusions. No passage is fabricated, paraphrased to distort, or stripped of context that would change its meaning. Where context matters, it is provided.

The commentary aims to be precise rather than polemical. Where Islamic scholars disagree among themselves, that disagreement is noted. Where a passage has a defensible reading, that reading is acknowledged before the problem with it is explained. The goal is not to mock but to examine — carefully, specifically, and without concession.

Readers who find a specific entry inaccurate, mistranslated, or missing essential context are encouraged to raise the objection at analyzingislam.com, where every entry in this volume is also published online and open for scrutiny.

How to use this book

Read it in order or jump directly to a category. Each entry stands on its own. The thematic chapters are self-contained — no prior entry is assumed when reading any later entry.

Use the Quran Verse Index at the back to locate entries by surah and verse. Use the Hadith Reference Index to locate entries by collection and number. Use the General Index to find entries touching a specific topic, person, or concept across all three sources.

Every entry references a specific passage. Verify before citing.
