Part I · Ch 1 — Abrogation
Part I · The Quran · Chapter 1
Abrogation
The Quran's own doctrine that Allah may replace a verse with something better or similar (2:106). Peaceful Meccan commands giving way to Medinan instructions on fighting, punishment, and the treatment of unbelievers. The text not only contains internal tensions — it provides the mechanism for resolving them in favour of whichever revelation came last.

Eschatology
Muslim's end-times collection — the most detailed account of the Dajjal across all hadith literature, the return of Jesus, the Mahdi's emergence, Gog and Magog flooding the earth and drinking whole lakes dry, the Hour arriving in an ordinary moment, and the fate of the soul in the grave (barzakh) between death and resurrection.

Part III · Sahih Muslim · Chapter 22
Strange &
Obscure
Muslim's unusual hadiths — the sun rising from the west as a sign of the Hour after which repentance will no longer be accepted, a conversation with a wolf that testified to Muhammad's prophethood, Adam's height at sixty cubits with humanity shrinking ever since, and various prophetic statements that companions preserved because they witnessed them.

Part III · Ch 23 — Animals
Part III · Sahih Muslim · Chapter 23
Animals
Dogs to be killed, especially black ones described as devils. The prohibition on selling dogs except for hunting. A woman who entered hellfire for locking a cat in a room until it died of hunger — she neither fed it nor let it eat from the ground. The talking wolf. Muslim's rulings on animals organised thematically, without Bukhari's tendency to repeat the same hadith across multiple chapters.

Part III · Ch 24 — Gross / Vile
Part III · Sahih Muslim · Chapter 24
Gross / Vile
Muslim's hadiths on bodily matters — the obligation to wash semen from clothing before prayer, detailed rules on istinja using stones (an odd number, not fewer than three), the prohibition on men urinating while standing according to some narrations, and the companion accounts of how the Prophet managed bodily functions and their ritual implications.
