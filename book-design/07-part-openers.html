The Quran
The central scripture of Islam, claimed by Muslims to be the eternal, unchanged word of Allah revealed to Muhammad over twenty-three years (c. 610–632 CE). Comprising 114 surahs and 6,236 verses, it is the highest authority in Islamic law and theology.

Sahih
al-Bukhari
Compiled by Muhammad ibn Ismail al-Bukhari (810–870 CE), who examined approximately 600,000 hadiths and retained 7,275. Considered by Sunni Islam to be the most authentic book after the Quran. Its rulings form the backbone of Sunni jurisprudence worldwide.

Sahih Muslim
Compiled by Muslim ibn al-Hajjaj (815–875 CE), a contemporary of al-Bukhari who examined approximately 300,000 hadiths and retained around 7,500. Ranked second only to Sahih al-Bukhari in Sunni tradition. Unlike Bukhari, it avoids repetition and is organized thematically by subject.
