What this is

Every entry cites a specific passage — either a Quranic verse or a hadith from one of the six canonical Sunni collections (Bukhari, Muslim, Abu Dawud, Tirmidhi, Nasa'i, Ibn Majah) — explains what it says in plain language, and builds a philosophical, historical, or moral argument for why it presents a problem. Where relevant, entries also provide the standard Muslim response and push back on it.

Quranic verses are quoted from the Saheeh International English translation, the Saudi-sanctioned mainstream Sunni edition. Hadiths are quoted from widely published English translations of the same collections Sunni jurisprudence itself treats as authoritative. Using Muslim-approved sources removes the easy dismissal of "hostile translation" or "fringe report."

About this project

The purpose and method behind the catalog.

The purpose

Every scripture that claims divine authorship invites philosophical scrutiny. This project applies that scrutiny to the Quran. The entries in the catalog identify textual, moral, historical, and logical problems across the book, and construct arguments that a thoughtful critic can use.

The sources

Quranic verses are quoted from the Saheeh International English translation — the Saudi-sanctioned mainstream Sunni edition used in mosques globally. This removes the objection that the quotes are from a fringe or hostile translation.

Hadiths are drawn from the six canonical Sunni collections (Bukhari, Muslim, Abu Dawud, Tirmidhi, Nasa'i, Ibn Majah) that Sunni jurisprudence itself treats as authoritative. Every citation can be cross-checked against the reader.

The method

Each entry: cites the primary source verbatim, explains what the passage says in plain language, builds a philosophical or moral argument for why it presents a problem, and — where relevant — provides the standard Muslim apologetical response and pushes back on it.

Entry strength ratings

Entries are rated Basic, Moderate, or Strong based on how resistant the argument is to standard apologetical responses. Strong entries are those where the apologetic response itself generates a deeper problem, or where answering the critique requires abandoning a core Islamic claim.

What this is not

This is not a polemic. It is an analytical catalog. Entries do not assert that all Muslims are bad people, that Islam has no positive features, or that any particular policy conclusion follows. They assert that specific passages present specific problems that honest inquiry should address.

FAQ

Answers to the most common questions about the project.

Isn't this just anti-Islamic propaganda?
Every entry is sourced to Muslim-approved texts — the Saheeh International translation and the six canonical hadith collections. The arguments are philosophical, historical, and textual. If a source is incorrectly cited, it can be verified and corrected. Propaganda doesn't cite its sources.
Why use only Sunni sources?
The catalog focuses on mainstream Sunni Islam because that represents the largest Muslim denomination globally and the tradition with the most developed canonical textual corpus. Using the sources Sunni jurisprudence itself treats as authoritative eliminates the objection that we're using fringe or hostile texts.
What does "Strong" on an entry mean?
Strong entries are those where the apologetic response itself generates a deeper problem, or where answering the critique requires abandoning a core Islamic claim. They're the arguments most resistant to standard deflection techniques — the cases worth studying first.
Is a Muslim response included?
Where a standard apologetical response exists, many entries include it and address it directly. The goal is not to misrepresent the Muslim position but to engage the strongest version of it.
Do I need an account to use the site?
No. The catalog, all readers, and the compare tool are open to everyone. An account is only required for the Build tool — saving collections, writing private notes, and sharing builds.
Can I cite entries from this site?
Every entry cites its primary source (chapter and verse). You should cite the primary source directly — not this site. The entry gives you the reference; the reader lets you verify it. Verify before citing.
How are entry strength ratings assigned?
Ratings are assigned based on how resistant the argument is to the full range of known Muslim apologetical responses. Basic entries have weaker primary arguments or more available Islamic responses. Strong entries require abandoning a core Islamic claim to refute.
