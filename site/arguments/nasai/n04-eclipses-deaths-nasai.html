Argument 4 of 20 · Sunan an-Nasā'ī

Eclipses Portend Deaths of Great People (Cosmological Error)

Nasa'i 1571 / 1578 (parallels Abu Dawud 1144)
The text
Nasa'i 1571 — Records the eclipse-prayer hadith. Muhammad performed eclipse prayer when the sun was eclipsed and rejected the popular belief that eclipses signal a great person's death. Nasa'i 1578 — Parallel hadith with similar content. The Nasa'i preservation is part of the broader cross-collection record on eclipses (parallel to Abu Dawud 1144, treated under entry d14).
Context

Nasa'i 1571 / 1578 preserves the same eclipse hadith as Abu Dawud 1144 (entry d14) and Bukhari 1059. The Nasa'i version provides independent chain attestation for the teaching that:

1. Eclipses are not caused by deaths of great persons (correcting folk belief). 2. Eclipses are 'signs from Allah' (introducing alternative metaphysical framing). 3. Muslims should perform special eclipse prayer (ṣalāt al-kusūf).

The historical occasion is well-attested. Modern eclipse calculations confirm that an annular solar eclipse occurred on 27 January 632 CE — the same day Muhammad's infant son Ibrahim died — and was visible across the Arabian peninsula. The coincidence triggered the popular belief that the eclipse was caused by Ibrahim's death, prompting Muhammad's correction. Ibn Kathir records the event in his chronicle Al-Bidaya wa'l-Nihaya (The Beginning and the End), noting that when the sun was eclipsed on the day Ibrahim died, observers concluded that God was marking his condolence to the Prophet, and that Muhammad responded by going to the mosque and instructing: "The sun and the moon do not eclipse because of the death or life of someone." Ibn Hajar al-'Asqalani, in his Fath al-Bari (his authoritative commentary on Sahih al-Bukhari), treats the eclipse narratives within Kitab al-Kusuf (the Book of Eclipses) and confirms that the correction of the folk belief was the direct occasion for establishing the eclipse prayer as a ritual institution. Al-Nawawi's al-Minhaj bi Sharh Sahih Muslim (his multi-volume commentary on Sahih Muslim, covering the Book of Prayer — Eclipses) similarly treats the ṣalāt al-kusūf as an operative ruling derived from the Prophet's response to the eclipse, anchoring the "signs" framing in mainstream Sunni jurisprudence.

The substantive issues are addressed in entry d14. The Nasa'i entry adds:

1. The Sunan-genre legal preservation. Nasa'i preserves the eclipse-prayer ruling in the prayer-chapter section, confirming its operative role in classical Islamic ritual practice.

2. Cross-collection consistency. The eclipse-prayer ruling and its underlying cosmological framework are preserved across multiple canonical collections — making the teaching part of mainstream Islamic doctrine. Sahih Muslim's parallel eclipse-prayer hadiths (Muslim 904a, 907a, 911a) carry the same formulation: "the sun and the moon are two signs among the signs of Allah" (āyatāni min āyāt Allāh) — a phrase that, as al-Nawawi's al-Minhaj makes plain, generates a standing legal obligation to pray whenever either body is eclipsed. Jonathan A.C. Brown, in his academic survey Hadith: Muhammad's Legacy in the Medieval and Modern World (Oneworld, 2009), explains that rulings attested across the Kutub al-Sitta — the six canonical Sunni collections, of which Nasa'i's Sunan is one — carry the highest degree of cross-chain endorsement in classical hadith methodology; the eclipse-prayer ruling qualifies on that criterion.

3. The persistent practice. The eclipse prayer continues to be performed by observant Muslims globally. Modern educational materials in conservative Muslim communities present the eclipse-prayer doctrine as substantive religious teaching.

4. The 'sign' interpretation. Modern Saudi religious authorities have invoked the 'sign' interpretation when major astronomical events occur. The framework treats predictable celestial phenomena as religiously meaningful occasions, retaining a metaphysical residue from pre-modern cosmology.

The analysis from entry d14 applies fully here.

Premises
  1. P1. Nasa'i 1571 / 1578 records the eclipse-prayer hadith with content paralleling Abu Dawud 1144 and Bukhari 1059.
  2. P2. Muhammad's correction (rejecting the death-cause folk belief) was a partial advance over Arabian superstition.
  3. P3. The 'signs from Allah' replacement framing is itself a metaphysical-religious interpretation, not natural-causation cosmology.
  4. P4. The hadith generates the eclipse prayer as religious obligation, anchoring the metaphysical framing in ongoing practice.
  5. P5. Modern astronomy demonstrates that eclipses are predictable celestial events caused by alignment of sun, moon, and earth.
  6. P6. The 'signs' interpretation continues to operate in conservative Islamic jurisprudence.
  7. P7. A correction that replaces folk belief with another metaphysical framework is partial advance, not divine teaching about the natural world. (See entry d14.)
Conclusion

Nasa'i 1571 / 1578 reinforces the eclipse-prayer doctrine with cross-collection attestation. The Nasa'i preservation places the teaching firmly in mainstream Islamic ritual practice. The 'signs from Allah' framing remains operative in modern conservative Islamic interpretation. (See entry d14 for substantive analysis.)

Common Muslim responses · with counter-responses
Common Muslim response · 1

Muhammad's correction was scientifically accurate to the extent possible in his time — the 'signs' framing is theological, not cosmological.

Counter-response

The framing generates ritual obligations tied to the natural events, implying more than abstract theological meaning. (See entry d14.)

Common Muslim response · 2

The eclipse prayer is a beautiful spiritual practice — natural events as occasions for reflection.

Counter-response

Adding spiritual dimension is fine, but the practice is anchored in the 'signs' interpretation, not in general celestial-event reflection. (See entry d14.)

Common Muslim response · 3

Modern Saudi astronomers do both — accurate prediction and eclipse prayer — without conflict.

Counter-response

The lack of conflict is achieved by treating the religious framing as supplementary spiritual practice rather than substantive cosmology. (See entry d14.)

Common Muslim response · 4

Muhammad's correction was a major advance — credit him with rejecting the folk belief.

Counter-response

The credit is real, but the standard for divine teaching is higher than 'partial correction.' (See entry d14.)

Common Muslim response · 5

Modern Muslim education integrates science and religion — eclipses as natural events with religious significance.

Counter-response

The integration is functional but conceptually unstable. (See entry d14.)
